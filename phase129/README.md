# Phase 129 — Lazy structural-certificate evaluation (8 Nov 2026)

**Question.** Does on-demand ("lazy") evaluation of the structural retained-exactness certificate (Lemma 3,
structural cases) give a *reliable* improvement over MBR **without** the certificate — not merely over the
eager certificate of Phase 128?

**Pre-registered criterion** (stated before any timing run): reliable improvement iff the median paired time
ratio no-certificate/lazy has a 95% bootstrap CI above 1 in a majority of the 15 dataset × update-kind
settings AND no setting has its CI entirely below 1.

**Verdict: NOT a reliable improvement.** CI > 1 in 7/15 settings (all synthetic), CI < 1 in 6/15 (all six
real-data settings, 0.90–0.95×), ambiguous in 2/15. On the seven 6-hour real-data streams of Table 6 the lazy
certificate is 2.5–5.4% slower than no certificate on every stream (time ratio 0.949–0.976) (`results/seq_heart_D12_lazy_0.csv`).

## Variants (differ only in the structural certificate)
`MBRNoCert` (certificate off) · `MBREager` (Phase 128, whole-cache scan, timed) · `MBRLazy` (on demand) · fresh `BnB`.
Implementation: `mbr_lazy.py`. Lazy mechanism: eligible old exact entries become Lemma-2 lower bounds (as in
no-cert) and are marked pending; when the search reaches a pending state the untouched condition is checked for
that state's old sub-policy only (memoised); untouched states and all untouched descendants found are promoted
to exact (Lemma 3). Correctness therefore rests on the same lemmas as the eager variant.

## Correctness (`test_lazy.py`)
11,550 updates in four runs (mixed, feasible-biased, two structural-heavy), all three variants equal to the
exhaustive DP; 1,908 also vs the exact-rational oracle; **state-level**: 41,765 lazy exact entries and 47,953
stored bounds checked against ground truth, 0 violations (1,803 promoted entries in the three runs that counted
promotions). Includes 6,559 failed replays (budget +∞), 240 feasible→infeasible and 146 infeasible→feasible
transitions. Every benchmark trial (630; 209 with failed replay, 49 with an infeasible updated model) was also checked: exhaustive DP on 570, fresh BnB on the remaining 60 syn32 trials.

## Results (`results/lazy_summary.csv`, raw per-trial CSVs `results/lazy_*.csv`)
See `results_table.md` (generated by `analyze_lazy.py`).

Key observations
1. **Synthetic:** lazy beats no-cert by 1.24–2.71× in 7/9 settings (largest for delay increases, growing with
   size); region shrinking at 24/36 and 32/44 is ambiguous. Lazy beats eager everywhere (1.17–1.92×) and uses
   less peak memory than eager.
2. **Real data (Cleveland):** lazy loses to no-cert in all 6 settings (0.90–0.95×, CIs below 1), although its
   certificate check is cheap (1–6% of update time vs 14–65% for eager).
3. **Why:** on the real instance Lemma 2 already makes the residual search near-minimal; the certificate can
   remove only 0.4–4.4% of no-cert expansions. Any implementation therefore has a ceiling of a few per cent,
   below the fixed per-update cost of marking pending entries and intercepting search calls.
4. Zero-search answers: identical for eager and lazy (e.g. 43/60 synthetic delay updates; 1–23/40 real),
   never for no-cert — the certificate is effective at *answering* updates without search, but on the real
   instance answering via Lemma 2 bounds is already almost as cheap.
5. Memory: lazy peak below eager in 14/15 settings; on real data lazy peak exceeds no-cert (pending marks).

## Critical novelty assessment
* **Lazy, demand-driven validation of cached results is established.** Adapton (Hammer, Khoo, Hicks & Foster,
  PLDI 2014, pp. 156–166, doi:10.1145/2594291.2594324) splits change propagation into a dirtying phase and
  demand-driven re-evaluation, re-evaluating only what an observer forces — the lazy certificate is this
  pattern specialised to Lemma 3 (pending marks ≈ dirty marks; on-demand untouched check ≈ forced re-evaluation).
  LPA*/D* Lite process inconsistent vertices only when the search needs them; DynamicHS (Rodler 2023) uses a
  lazy tree-update policy; lazy edge evaluation is standard in motion planning (e.g. L-GLS, Lim et al. 2024).
* **Incremental AND/OR search is established** (Minimax LPA*, Likhachev & Koenig 2003), as is dependency repair
  (Ramalingam & Reps 1996). The certificate itself is an immediate consequence of Theorem 0 + Lemma 2.
* **What is specific here** is only the combination: an untouched-policy check for structural constraint
  updates (delay, safety, decision region) in expected-cost AND/OR search, evaluated on demand. This is an
  application of known principles, not a new algorithmic principle, and runtime gains alone would not change
  that assessment.
* **Conclusion:** the lazy certificate does not establish a defensible algorithmic contribution for ESWA: it is
  not a new principle, and it does not reliably beat the established-components baseline on the real case
  study. In line with the instruction not to propose minor variations without justification: the expansion
  ceiling (≤ 4.4% on real data) shows that no further implementation refinement of this certificate can change
  the real-data verdict.
